Everyday cycle tracking

For knowing where you are in your cycle, and what tends to come with it.

Log a period in two taps. Your expected period shows as a range with what it rests on, and over time your symptoms sort themselves by phase. On the pill, ring or patch, Dottie counts from your pack schedule instead of guessing. On a hormonal IUD, implant or shot it makes no prediction at all and watches for changes instead.

Trying to conceive Plus

For when you want to time things.

Dottie centers your fertile window, adds LH and test logging, and estimates ovulation from temperature read from Apple Health. The window appears after three logged cycles. It is an estimate for timing, not birth control.

Pregnancy

For when you are pregnant.

A week counter, and a due date you can update if your care provider gives you a different one. Your records, a short note for each week, and the doctor visit summary. No development content and no new app. If the pregnancy ends, one line turns everything pregnancy related off, and you choose whether to resume cycle tracking now or pause for a while.

After a pregnancy

For the months after a pregnancy, however it ended.

Predictions stay off until you log a new period. When cycles come back, Dottie shows ranges that narrow as new ones are logged. There is no schedule your body is meant to keep.

“Cycles coming back after a pregnancy often vary. The range narrows as new ones are logged.”

Changing cycles

For perimenopause and menopause.

When cycles vary, Dottie shows a range of weeks instead of a date, and says why. Hot flashes and the other transition symptoms have their own place in the log. A quiet counter keeps the months since your last period, and if bleeding returns after 12 months, Dottie says plainly that doctors recommend getting it checked. Plus adds dated ranges and a monthly symptoms score.

“12 months without a recorded period.”

For parents

If someone in your family has just started her periods, this is the part of Dottie written for her, and here is what it is and isn't.

A wide band is an honest one. First Cycles keeps it wide while her cycles settle.

What First Cycles is
A mode she turns on by choosing “My periods started recently” when she sets up Dottie, or later in Settings. Predictions stay wide while her cycles settle, and Dottie tells her that is normal where she is. Explanations assume she is new to all this.
What it doesn't show
No fertile window, no ovulation estimates and no conception tracking. No “irregular” label, because the first years are meant to vary. The adult reading room stays out of it. She gets five short reads written for her age: what is happening in your body, the first couple of years, period products and using them safely, cramps and what helps, and what is worth mentioning to someone.
What it costs
Nothing. Everything in First Cycles is free, and there is nothing for sale inside it. No upgrade prompts and no locked cards.
Where her data goes
Nowhere. Dottie has no account to create and no server to send anything to. What she logs stays on her phone, encrypted.
If she wants to talk to someone
Where an adult would be pointed to a doctor, First Cycles points to a trusted adult, school nurse, or doctor. It names no one in particular.
Age
Dottie is for ages 13 and up.
It's hers
First Cycles is a mode she chooses, and she can turn it off in Settings when she is ready. Dottie has no parental view. What she logs is on her phone and nowhere else.

Safety features are free for everyone.

Some people track because home isn't safe. These are built in, and none of them is paid.

  • App lock

    A 4-digit PIN or Face ID. Dottie locks the moment it leaves the screen, with no grace period. Wrong guesses slow down, and nothing is wiped.

  • Decoy PIN

    A second PIN for when someone else picks up your phone. It keeps what you logged private.

  • No account

    Nothing to sign up for and nothing to sign in to. There is no profile anywhere that says you use a period tracker.

  • Delete all data

    Two taps in Settings erase everything, including the keys that lock it. There is no server copy, so only a backup you saved yourself can bring it back.

  • Reminders that keep quiet

    Reminders are made on your phone. The lock screen shows the word “Reminder” and a plain line like “A quick check-in for today?”. Nothing on it says what you track.

  • Not a medical device

    Dottie is not a medical device. It offers information only, not medical advice or contraception. It doesn't diagnose, and a late day gets calm, neutral information rather than a conclusion.

What “private” means here.

Many apps are built around an account and a server. Dottie was built without either, so there is nothing to leak. This is the whole list.

Account
None. Nothing to sign up for. Dottie doesn't know who you are.
Cloud
None. Your records live in an encrypted database on your iPhone. A backup goes only where you send it.
Analytics
None. No usage tracking and no crash reports. Dottie has no code that connects to the internet.
Cookies, on this site
None. No cookies and no trackers, and it loads nothing from anyone else.

A little about why Dottie exists

Dottie started with someone very close to me.

My partner was 30 when she went into early menopause. It was a confusing time, and finding the right support wasn't easy.

We looked for an app that could help her understand and track what she was going through, but nothing felt right. Some were overwhelming, filled with features she didn't need. Others were too limited to reflect what she was experiencing.

I wanted to make something calm and simple, where she could track what was happening without feeling overwhelmed, and where her personal information stayed personal.

What started as an app for her slowly grew. I realized there might be other women, at every age and stage of life, looking for the same thing: a place to understand their own experiences without giving up their privacy.

That's why I made Dottie. It's a small project with a lot of heart behind it, and I hope it makes someone's day a little easier, the way I hoped it would for my partner.

If you have an idea, or something isn't working the way you'd like, I'd love to hear from you at hello@thedottie.app. There's no pressure to write, but your feedback helps shape what Dottie becomes.

Thank you for giving Dottie a place in your life.

— Rob

What does Dottie collect?

Nothing. There is no account, no server and no analytics. The App Store privacy label is the blank one. This website is the same: no cookies, no trackers, no third-party scripts or fonts.

What is free, and what is Plus?

Logging, the calendar with two months ahead, predictions, every safety feature, pregnancy mode, changing cycles, first cycles and the doctor visit summary are free. Your own history is free to see. Plus adds symptom timing and heads-ups, twelve months on the calendar, a fuller doctor visit summary, trying to conceive, Apple Health sync, the library and a recap after each cycle. Apple shows the price in your region.

Can my teenager use Dottie?

Yes, from 13 up. First Cycles is the mode for periods that started recently, and all of it is free with nothing for sale inside. There is more in For parents.

What if I lose my phone?

Export an encrypted backup whenever you like, with a passphrase you choose, and keep it where you keep your own files. Restore it on a new phone from Settings or from the lock screen. If you forget the passphrase, the file is gone. There is no reset, because there is no server.

Can I bring my history from another app?

If it is in Apple Health, yes: a one-time import of menstrual flow and spotting, read on the device. Days you've already logged stay as they are. Dottie never writes to Apple Health.

Does Dottie prevent pregnancy?

No. Dottie is information only. It is not medical advice or contraception. Fertile-window estimates are for awareness only, and they stay off on hormonal methods.

Is Dottie a medical device?

No. Dottie is not a medical device. It offers information only, not medical advice or contraception. It only knows what you log, so it can miss things. If something doesn't feel right, trust that and get it checked.

Who made Dottie?

Just me, Rob. Dottie is a one-person project. It's paid for by the people who choose Plus, not by advertisers, so there's nothing to sell.
