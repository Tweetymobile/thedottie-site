Everyday cycle tracking

For knowing where you are in your cycle, and what tends to come with it.

Log a period in two taps, with flow from spotting to flooding. Your next period shows as a range with what it rests on, and over time your symptoms sort themselves by phase.

Tell Dottie when you start or stop a birth control method and it keeps a history of each one. On the combined pill, ring or patch, it counts from your pack schedule instead of guessing. On the mini-pill, a hormonal IUD or an implant it makes no period prediction, and if your bleeding becomes regular it offers to predict when that starts next. On the shot it predicts nothing and watches for changes instead.

Trying to conceive Plus

For when you want to time things.

Dottie centers your fertile window, adds LH and test logging, and estimates ovulation from your temperature, typed in or read from Apple Health. The window appears after three logged cycles.

Pregnancy

For when you are pregnant.

A week counter, and a due date you can update if your care provider gives you a different one. Your records, a short note for each week, and the doctor visit summary. No development content and no new app.

If you log bleeding, Dottie says the same day that it should be checked. If you turned pregnancy on by mistake, you can undo it. If the pregnancy ends, one line turns everything pregnancy related off, and you choose whether to resume cycle tracking now or pause for a while.

“Take whatever time you need. Dottie will be here, exactly as you left it.”

After a pregnancy

For the months after a pregnancy, however it ended.

For the first six weeks, bleeding is recorded as bleeding after pregnancy, not as a period, and if it is heavy Dottie tells you when to get medical help. Predictions stay off until you log a new period. If none has come after three months, Dottie mentions once that it is worth checking with a doctor.

When cycles come back, Dottie shows ranges that narrow as new ones are logged. There is no schedule your body is meant to keep.

“Cycles coming back after a pregnancy often vary. The range narrows as new ones are logged.”

Changing cycles

For perimenopause and menopause.

When cycles vary a lot, Dottie shows a range of weeks instead of a date, and says why. Hot flashes and the other transition symptoms have their own place in the log. A quiet counter keeps the days since your last period, and if bleeding returns after 12 months, Dottie says plainly that doctors recommend getting it checked.

Plus shows that range as dates, and adds a card counting the days you logged each transition symptom, this month and last.

“12 months without a recorded period.”

For parents

If someone in your family has just started her periods, this is the part of Dottie written for her, and here is what it is and isn't.

“Period could start Nov 20 – Dec 8”. A wide range is an honest one. First Cycles keeps it wide while her cycles settle.

What First Cycles is
A mode she turns on by choosing “My periods started recently” when she sets up Dottie, or later in Settings. Predictions stay wide while her cycles settle, and Dottie tells her that is normal where she is. Explanations assume she is new to all this.
What it doesn't show
No fertile window, no ovulation estimates and no conception tracking. No “irregular” label, because the first years are meant to vary. The adult reading room stays out of it. She gets five short reads written for her age: what is happening in your body, the first couple of years, period products and using them safely, cramps and what helps, and what is worth mentioning to someone.
What it costs
Nothing. Everything in First Cycles is free, and there is nothing for sale inside it. No upgrade prompts and no locked cards.
Where her data goes
Nowhere. Dottie has no account to create and no server to send anything to. What she logs stays on her device, encrypted.
If she wants to talk to someone
Where an adult would be pointed to a doctor, First Cycles points to a trusted adult, school nurse, or doctor. It names no one in particular.
Age
Dottie is for ages 13 and up.
It's hers
First Cycles is a mode she chooses, and she can turn it off in Settings when she is ready. Dottie has no parental view. What she logs is on her device and nowhere else.

Safety features are free for everyone.

Some people track because home isn't safe. These are built in, and none of them is paid.

  • App lock

    An optional 4-digit PIN, with Face ID on top if you want it. Once it's on, Dottie locks the moment it leaves the screen, with no grace period. Wrong guesses slow down, and nothing is wiped.

  • Decoy PIN

    A second PIN for when someone else picks up your device. It keeps what you logged private.

  • No account

    Nothing to sign up for and nothing to sign in to. Dottie keeps no profile of you.

  • Delete all data

    Two taps in Settings erase everything, including the keys that lock it. There is no server copy, so only a backup you saved yourself can bring it back.

  • Reminders that keep quiet

    Reminders are made on your device. The lock screen shows the word “Reminder” and a plain line like “A quick check-in for today?”. The one the day before a symptom usually starts says only “Tomorrow may be worth a note.” Nothing on it says what you track.

  • Not a medical device

    Dottie is not a medical device. It offers information only, not medical advice or contraception. It doesn't diagnose, and a late day gets calm, neutral information rather than a conclusion. On eight subjects, such as bleeding in pregnancy or flooding, it says plainly that a doctor should hear about it.

A summary you could hand over.

A PDF of your cycle history and symptom patterns, created on this device from your own data.

Heavy flow and flooding are listed by date, one line for each period, and each birth control method has a line of its own.

It has to be readable by your doctor, so the PDF isn't encrypted. Dottie deletes its copy after you share it. Any copies you save or send are under your control. You can add your name and a note when you create it, and nothing you type there is saved.

Plus adds a cycle table, flagged patterns, a day-by-day symptom map and health details you can type in, such as conditions and medications.

A little about why Dottie exists

Dottie started with someone very close to me.

My partner was 30 when she went into early menopause. It was a confusing time, and finding the right support wasn't easy.

We looked for an app that could help her understand and track what she was going through, but nothing felt right. Some were overwhelming, filled with features she didn't need. Others were too limited to reflect what she was experiencing.

I wanted to make something calm and simple, where she could track what was happening without feeling overwhelmed, and where her personal information stayed personal.

What started as an app for her slowly grew. I realized there might be other women, at every age and stage of life, looking for the same thing: a place to understand their own experiences without giving up their privacy.

That's why I made Dottie. It's a small project with a lot of heart behind it, and I hope it makes someone's day a little easier, the way I hoped it would for my partner.

If you have an idea, or something isn't working the way you'd like, I'd love to hear from you at hello@thedottie.app. There's no pressure to write, but your feedback helps shape what Dottie becomes.

Thank you for giving Dottie a place in your life.

— Rob

What does Dottie collect?

Nothing. There is no account, no server and no analytics. This website is the same: no cookies, no trackers, no third-party scripts or fonts.

What is free, and what is Plus?

Logging, predictions for the weeks ahead, your whole history, the doctor visit summary and every safety feature are free. Plus adds what Dottie learns from your logs, such as symptom patterns and a recap after each cycle. Both lists are in What's free, and what's Plus. Apple shows the price in your region.

What happens if I stop paying for Plus?

Nothing you logged or synced is lost. The Plus features pause, and they come back on if you rejoin. Everything that was free stays as it was.

Can my teenager use Dottie?

Yes, from 13 up. First Cycles is the mode for periods that started recently, and all of it is free with nothing for sale inside. There is more in For parents.

What if I lose my device?

Export an encrypted backup whenever you like, with a passphrase you choose, and keep it where you keep your own files. Restore it on a new device from Settings or from the lock screen. If you forget the passphrase, the file is gone. There is no reset, because there is no server.

Can I bring my history from another app?

If it is in Apple Health, yes: a one-time import of menstrual flow and spotting, read on the device. Days you've already logged stay as they are. Dottie never writes to Apple Health. The import is free. Reading your sleep, temperature, weight and blood pressure from Apple Health as you go is part of Plus.

Does Dottie prevent pregnancy?

No. Dottie is information only. It is not medical advice or contraception. Fertile-window estimates are for awareness only, and they stay off on hormonal methods.

Is Dottie a medical device?

No. Dottie is not a medical device. It offers information only, not medical advice or contraception. It only knows what you log, so it can miss things. If something doesn't feel right, trust that and get it checked.

Who made Dottie?

Just me, Rob. Dottie is a one-person project. It's paid for by the people who choose Plus, not by advertisers, so there's nothing to sell.
